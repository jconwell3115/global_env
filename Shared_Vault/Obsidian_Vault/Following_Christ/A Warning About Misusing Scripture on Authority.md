Devotional: A Warning About Misusing Scripture on Authority

Scripture Reading: Romans 13:1-7; 1 Peter 2:13-17

Throughout history, verses like Romans 13 and 1 Peter 2 that command submission to authority have often been misused to justify authoritarian and oppressive regimes. Such misuse serves those in power rather than the vulnerable and distorts the Gospel’s call to love and freedom.[](https://www.gotquestions.org/subject-to-the-governing-authorities.html)

From the horrors of the Nazi regime to the defense of slavery, these scriptures have many times been twisted to uphold injustice rather than God’s justice. During Nazi Germany, certain Christian groups and theologians manipulated biblical passages, especially Romans 13, to promote absolute obedience to Hitler and justify horrific acts including racial persecution and genocide. Similarly, the same scriptures were used historically to defend the institution of slavery, cloaking oppression and racial injustice in a false cloak of divine approval. These abuses illustrate how vulnerable sacred texts are to being distorted when interpreted through the lens of political power and social prejudice, resulting in grave violations of human dignity and betrayal of the Gospel’s true message.

In a democratic country like the United States, true authority does not rest solely in elected officials or government leaders. Instead, authority is grounded in the Constitution—the supreme law of the land—and ultimately in the people, who hold sovereign power. This means that speaking out about elected officials or government actions is not equivalent to speaking against the ultimate authority God has appointed in the United States. The Constitution provides a system of checks and balances through three separate branches—legislative, executive, and judicial—that share and limit power to protect the rights and welfare of the people. Holding elected officials accountable, calling out abuses, and engaging in lawful dissent is part of honoring the constitutional authority given by God to the people, not rebellion against divine order.

​The Jewish leaders had strictly commanded the apostles not to teach or preach in the name of Jesus. They accused Paul and the other apostles of filling Jerusalem with their teaching and claimed they were responsible for bringing "this Man's blood" upon them, referring to Jesus. Essentially, they demanded that Paul stop proclaiming Jesus as the risen Messiah and cease spreading the gospel, fearing the growing influence of the Christian movement and its threat to their religious authority.  To which the very author of Romans 13, Paul,  declared his highest allegiance when he said to the Jewish council, "We must obey God rather than men!" (Acts 5:29).

Respect for authority, therefore, is not blind submission. It requires honoring legitimate institutions while holding all people accountable to God's higher law, especially elected officials who claim Christ with their lips but blatantly dishonor Him through injustice, hypocrisy, pride, or abuse. Christians have a responsibility to call out such distortions whenever leaders misrepresent Christ’s teachings for personal gain or political power. Let us guard against using scripture to control or oppress others. Instead, may we follow Jesus, who challenged unjust authority and revealed God’s kingdom through humility and truth.

Prayer:  
Lord, open our eyes to the misuse of Your Word and the hypocrisy hidden behind false authority. Help us to stand for justice, speak truth in love, and submit only in ways that honor You. Teach us to follow Christ faithfully, refle cting His humility, compassion, and courage. Amen.
