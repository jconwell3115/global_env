### Devotional: Serving God Above All Authorities

Many movements in history have misused verses like Romans 13, which urge obedience to governing authorities, to justify oppressive and authoritarian rule. From Nazi Germany's German Christian movement proclaiming Hitler as God's appointed leader to justify horrific persecution, to 19th-century American slaveholders demanding submission from the enslaved, to apartheid South Africa and colonial regimes enforcing racial hierarchy under biblical claims, scripture was distorted to silence dissent and uphold injustice. More recently, the MAGA movement has similarly invoked Romans 13 to demand unquestioning loyalty to political leaders, endorsing policies and rhetoric that suppress opposition and undermine justice.

Yet true Christian teaching calls us to **serve God above human authorities**, recognizing that all earthly power is limited and subordinate to God's higher law and justice. The prophet Micah reminds us:

> "He has shown you, O mortal, what is good. And what does the Lord require of you? To act justly and to love mercy and to walk humbly with your God." (Micah 6:8, NIV)

Jesus also taught a servant-hearted posture toward authority:

> "You know that those who are regarded as rulers of the Gentiles lord it over them... But it shall not be so among you. Instead, whoever wants to become great among you must be your servant... The Son of Man did not come to be served, but to serve." (Mark 10:42-45)

We are called to serve God with gladness and humility:

> "Serve the Lord with gladness; come before his presence with singing." (Psalm 100:2)  
> "Whatever you do, work heartily, as for the Lord and not for men." (Colossians 3:23)  
> "Choose this day whom you will serve... as for me and my house, we will serve the Lord." (Joshua 24:15)

Serving God means acting justly, loving mercy, and walking humbly even in the face of human authorities who demand otherwise. Obedience to government is not absolute; it must be measured by God’s call to righteousness and compassion. Christians are called to discernment and courage — serving God faithfully above all earthly powers.

***

This devotional calls believers to reflect on the true heart of biblical submission—one that honors justice, mercy, and humility—contrasted with historical and modern abuses of scripture to justify oppression and authoritarianism.
