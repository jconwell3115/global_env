# Devotional: Truth, Mercy, and Honoring God  

Throughout history, groups have risen to defend tradition, preserve identity, and seek power. In Jesus' day, the Pharisees were respected for their commitment to faith and spiritual discipline, yet sometimes their zeal became a burden, placing heavy expectations on others and fostering division rather than healing. Jesus challenged their legalism, not to destroy, but to invite them—and all of us—back to the heart of God: justice, mercy, and humble love[3][5][4].

Today, the actions and positions of movements like MAGA can be compared to the Pharisees. Pointing out similar patterns: the emphasis on national identity, concern for tradition, and the temptation to judge or exclude others that don't look like, act like or think like they do. But if every era can fall into these traps, the point is not to condemn, but to ask: Where might we, too, risk missing the deeper call of Christ? Jesus’ strong words were aimed not at all Pharisees or one movement, but at the underlying pride and hypocrisy that can touch any community—including ours[3][5].

When Jesus spoke up, the people long weighed down by religious or social control found relief and hope. They saw in Him a champion of those excluded and oppressed, a teacher who brought dignity and new possibility[5]. His message lifts us still, calling everyone—across political or religious lines—toward repentance, mercy, and reconciliation.

Jesus invites us to clean "the inside of the cup" (Matthew 23:26), to care sincerely for those burdened by our traditions, and to let love, honesty, and compassion guide our steps.

***

## Prayer

Lord God,  
Thank You for sending Jesus to speak truth—not just to the Pharisees, but to every heart and community tempted by pride, exclusion, or self-righteousness. Give us grace to examine ourselves, to listen with humility, and to return to Your call for justice and mercy. Make us builders of unity, healing, and hope, offering dignity and compassion wherever division or burdens exist.  
In Jesus’ name, Amen.
